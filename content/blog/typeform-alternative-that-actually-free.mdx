## Why Typeform's Free Plan Falls Short in 2025

Before jumping to alternatives, it's worth understanding exactly what you're dealing with. Typeform's free plan allows:

- **10 responses per month** across all your forms
- Unlimited questions and form creation
- No removal of Typeform branding
- Limited integrations (Zapier, Google Sheets, and Slack require a Plus plan or higher, starting at ~$29/month)

Once you hit the 10-response cap, your form goes into Private mode automatically. It reactivates the following month, or the moment you upgrade. For anyone running a real lead gen form, a job application, or a customer feedback survey, this is a dealbreaker.

## The 5 Best Free Typeform Alternatives in 2025

### 1. Google Forms, Best for Simplicity and Zero Cost

Google Forms is the obvious starting point, and for good reason. It's completely free, forever, with no response limits, no branding restrictions, and no hidden tiers.

You get conditional logic, file uploads, multiple question types, and tight integration with Google Sheets for real-time data collection. For educators, small teams, and anyone already in the Google ecosystem, it's hard to argue against.

**What it lacks:** Google Forms is functional, not beautiful. There's no one-question-at-a-time conversational flow, and customization is minimal. If design matters to your brand, you'll feel the difference immediately.

**Best for:** Internal surveys, event RSVPs, quick data collection, anyone already using Google Workspace.

---

### 2. Tally, Best Free Typeform-Like Experience

Tally is the closest thing to Typeform that doesn't cost anything. It offers a genuinely conversational, one-question-at-a-time interface, the same format that made Typeform popular, but on a free plan that includes:

- Unlimited forms
- Unlimited responses
- Conditional logic
- File uploads
- Payment collection (via Stripe)

The free tier is remarkably generous. Tally monetizes through a paid plan for teams and advanced branding, but most individual users and small businesses won't need it.

**What it lacks:** White-labeling and advanced team collaboration features require upgrading. Tally's design customization is slightly less flexible than Typeform's.

**Best for:** Creators, indie makers, and anyone who loved Typeform's UX but not its price.

---

### 3. JotForm, Best for Templates and Complex Forms

JotForm has one of the largest template libraries of any form builder, with over 10,000 ready-to-use templates covering everything from medical intake forms to event registrations. The drag-and-drop builder is intuitive, and the free plan is functional, though more limited than Tally or Google Forms.

**Free plan includes:**

- Up to 5 forms
- 100 submissions per month
- 100MB storage
- JotForm branding on forms

It's not unlimited, but 100 submissions/month is a meaningful step up from Typeform's 10. And the template depth is unmatched if you need a head start on complex forms.

**Best for:** Users who need a specific form type fast and want to choose from a deep library of pre-built layouts.

---

### 4. Youform, Best for Unlimited Everything (Free)

Youform has quickly become one of the most talked-about free Typeform alternatives because of how much it offers without charging a cent. The free plan includes:

- Unlimited forms
- Unlimited submissions
- Conditional logic
- Digital signatures
- Integrations with third-party tools
- Conversational, one-question-at-a-time format

It mirrors Typeform's core experience almost feature-for-feature, making it one of the most direct replacements available.

**Best for:** Anyone who wants the full Typeform experience, flow, design, logic, with none of the response caps.

---

### 5. Fillout, Best for Automation and Workflow Integration

Fillout sits at the intersection of form building and workflow automation. It connects natively with tools like Notion, Airtable, and Google Sheets, and its free plan is generous enough for most small-scale use cases. Forms are clean, load fast, and support conditional logic without friction.

**Best for:** Developers, product teams, and no-code builders who want forms that feed directly into their existing tools and workflows.

## What to Look for in a Free Form Builder

Before committing to any tool, ask these questions:

- **What's the actual response cap?** Some tools advertise "free forever" but limit you to 50–100 responses before pushing you to upgrade.
- **Is branding removable?** Forms with another company's logo undermine your credibility.
- **Does it support conditional logic?** Branching questions are essential for anything beyond a basic survey.
- **How does it handle integrations?** Check whether Zapier, Notion, or Google Sheets connections require a paid plan.
- **Is it actively maintained?** A tool that hasn't shipped updates in 12 months is a tool that won't be around in 24.
